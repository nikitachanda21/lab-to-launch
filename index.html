Track 1 · Preparing for a PhD

A year-by-year roadmap

What to focus on at each stage of undergrad if you want a PhD to lead into the quantum industry. Timelines here follow the US system.

  1. Years 1–2

    Build your foundation

    • Treat grades as an investment. GPA shapes access to internships and fellowships, and founders we spoke with wish they had taken it more seriously.
    • Join a research group, even in a small role, and learn to document your work carefully.
    • Go to talks and club events in quantum to learn what the field looks like outside class.
  2. Year 3

    Do research well and know the field

    • Bring something real to every meeting with your mentor. Reliability builds trust faster than personality.
    • Follow quantum companies and notice what problems they are trying to solve.
    • Start a list of programs and advisors with strong ties to industry, startups or national labs.
  3. Summer before senior year

    Start applications early

    • Begin at the start of the summer. Once classes start, deadlines arrive faster than you expect.
    • Research each program carefully, and prepare fellowship applications such as the NSF GRFP.
    • Ask for recommendation letters from people who have seen your research up close.
  4. Senior year and after

    Choose deliberately

    • In the US, PhD students are usually paid, while master's students usually pay tuition. Factor that into your choice.
    • Not sure yet? A gap year working in a lab or company is a real option, not a detour.

    About the author

    Why I'm building Lab to Launch

    A physicist who writes, mapping the path from PhD to industry while walking it.

    Nikita Chanda

    I'm a physics student at UC Santa Barbara, minoring in professional writing with a focus on science communication. I've done research in quantum photonics at UCSB and at the Centre for Quantum Technologies at the National University of Singapore, and I serve as Marketing Officer for Quantum Computing Santa Barbara and as an officer in Womentum, UCSB's Women in Physics organization.

    I started Lab to Launch after an informational interview showed me how much of the path from a PhD into the quantum industry depends on conversations most students never get to have. I'm considering that path myself, so this site is my way of learning it while making it easier for others to follow.

    My logo puts my initials inside orbits, with a quill whose feather is made of sine and cosine waves. Light is a wave, and writing is how I make sense of it.
